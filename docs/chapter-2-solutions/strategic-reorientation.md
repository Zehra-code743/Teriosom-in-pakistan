# Section I: Strategic Reorientation and National Action Plan Review

The causal analysis in Chapter 1 documented how terrorism in Pakistan emerges from the interaction of geopolitical preconditions, domestic institutional weaknesses, and socio-economic marginalization. This section addresses the strategic framework required to translate diagnostic understanding into effective prevention policy.

## I.A Military Operations Assessment

Pakistan's military operations against terrorist sanctuaries represent significant achievements. Operation Zarb-e-Azb (June 2014) and Operation Radd-ul-Fasaad (February 2017) successfully dismantled the territorial control that militant organizations had established in the former Federally Administered Tribal Areas. These operations cleared physical sanctuaries, disrupted command structures, and degraded operational capacity. The reduction in terrorism-related fatalities from 2014 through 2020 demonstrates tactical effectiveness.

As Chapter 1's [synthesis](/docs/chapter-1-causes/synthesis#112-synthesis-of-findings) documented, however, military success against territorial control did not address structural preconditions enabling terrorism's persistence. Governance gaps, justice sector weaknesses, and socio-economic marginalization remain operative after kinetic operations conclude. The post-2021 escalation demonstrates that military clearance without concurrent institutional development creates conditions for militant resurgence once external precipitants activate latent vulnerabilities.

The evidence suggests a strategic transition: from military-led operations designed to eliminate territorial control toward civilian-led approaches focused on preventing radicalization and consolidating governance. This transition does not diminish military contributions but recognizes that sustainable security requires capabilities beyond the military's institutional mandate.

## I.B National Action Plan Review and Revision

The twenty-point National Action Plan, adopted in January 2015 following a devastating school attack in Peshawar, represented Pakistan's most comprehensive counter-terrorism framework. NAP encompassed military operations, judicial reforms, madrasah registration, counter-financing measures, and commitments to address governance vacuums.

Chapter 1's examination of [domestic institutional flaws](/docs/chapter-1-causes/domestic-flaws#161-political-instability-and-nap-failure) documented NAP's implementation record: only five to seven of twenty points achieved meaningful implementation by 2024. Military operations and military courts proceeded effectively, but most commitments saw partial or negligible progress.

**Madrasah Registration**: Only 30 to 40 percent of seminaries completed registration by 2023, preventing curriculum oversight and perpetuating educational environments that may facilitate radicalization.

**Counter-Narrative Development**: The commitment to counter extremist narratives produced few concrete outcomes. No sustained counter-narrative campaign achieved national reach, and digital platforms remained largely unaddressed.

**NACTA Coordination**: The National Counter Terrorism Authority suffered from inadequate staffing, unclear mandate, and political marginalization. Federal-provincial coordination remained weak, with intelligence sharing incomplete and jurisdictional disputes delaying responses.

### Recommendation 1: NAP Revision with Implementation Timelines

A revised NAP framework should retain the original twenty points while adding explicit implementation timelines, measurable indicators, and accountability mechanisms. Each commitment should specify responsible institutions, resource requirements, quarterly milestones, and consequences for non-compliance. Parliamentary oversight committees should receive regular implementation reports.

*Implementation Considerations*: Revision requires renewed political consensus among federal and provincial governments. Civil society organizations with monitoring capacity should participate in framework development to enhance external accountability.

### Recommendation 2: NACTA Institutional Strengthening

NACTA requires transformation into an empowered institution with clear mandate, adequate staffing, and operational capacity. The Authority should possess statutory authority to convene agencies, access intelligence products, and monitor implementation. Leadership appointments should reflect professional expertise with fixed tenures ensuring continuity.

*Implementation Considerations*: Strengthening requires legislative amendment, resource allocation matching expanded responsibilities, and recruitment of specialized staff. International technical assistance may accelerate capacity development while maintaining Pakistani institutional ownership.

## I.C Toward a Non-Kinetic Strategic Shift

The limitations identified above point toward strategic reorientation positioning civilian-led prevention as a co-equal pillar alongside security operations. This "whole-of-government" approach recognizes that sustainable counter-terrorism requires coordinated action across multiple domains, with civilian agencies assuming primary responsibility for prevention, deradicalization, and governance consolidation.

Chapter 1's synthesis identified [intervention points](/docs/chapter-1-causes/synthesis#identifying-intervention-points) across governance, justice, socio-economic, and ideological dimensions. Addressing these requires capabilities residing primarily in civilian institutions: provincial administrations, the judicial system, educational authorities, and development agencies.

### Recommendation 3: Civilian-Led CT Coordination Body

Pakistan should establish a civilian-led coordination body with representation from federal ministries, provincial governments, and security agencies. This body should develop prevention strategy, allocate resources, and monitor implementation. Provincial chief secretaries should participate to ensure federal strategy translates into provincial action, particularly in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan.

*Implementation Considerations*: Effective civilian coordination requires addressing the historical imbalance between military and civilian institutional capacity. Civilian agencies require capacity building and resource allocation commensurate with expanded responsibilities. The body should complement NACTA with clear delineation of strategic versus operational responsibilities.

### Recommendation 4: Civil Society Consultation Mechanism

Effective prevention requires engagement with communities, religious leaders, educators, and civil society organizations possessing local knowledge and implementation capacity. A formal consultation mechanism should provide structured channels for civil society input into prevention strategy, program design, and monitoring---including representation from women's organizations, youth groups, and community-based organizations in conflict-affected areas.

*Implementation Considerations*: Consultation requires establishing trust between government institutions and non-governmental actors. Clear protocols for information sharing, participant protection, and feedback incorporation will determine effectiveness. International experience confirms that genuine consultation yields significant dividends in program design and community acceptance.
