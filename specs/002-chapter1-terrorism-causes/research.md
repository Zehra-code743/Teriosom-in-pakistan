## Research Overview

This document consolidates research decisions, source frameworks, and analytical approaches for generating Chapter 1 content on the causes of terrorism in Pakistan.

## Key Research Decisions

### 1. Analytical Framework Selection

**Decision**: Adopt Martha Crenshaw's "Preconditions vs. Precipitants" framework as the primary theoretical lens.

**Rationale**:
- Widely accepted in terrorism studies literature
- Distinguishes between structural enabling factors (preconditions) and immediate triggers (precipitants)
- Avoids single-cause fallacies while maintaining analytical rigor
- Aligns with FR-002 and FR-004 requirements

**Alternatives Considered**:
- Root Causes Theory: Rejected as overly deterministic and politically contested
- Radicalization Pathway Models: Too focused on individual psychology; less suited for macro-level chapter
- Rational Choice Theory: Useful but insufficient for ideological/structural factors

### 2. Temporal Scope Definition

**Decision**: Primary focus on post-2021 surge with historical context extending to 1979 Soviet-Afghan War.

**Rationale**:
- August 2021 Taliban takeover is the key watershed event for current surge
- Soviet War legacy (1979-1989) established foundational militant infrastructure
- Provides sufficient depth without scope creep
- Aligns with spec Assumptions section

**Alternatives Considered**:
- Pre-1947 Partition context: Rejected as out of scope per spec
- 2001-2021 only: Insufficient historical depth for Soviet legacy analysis
- Extended Cold War framing: Too broad; dilutes focus on Pakistan-specific factors

### 3. Data Source Hierarchy

**Decision**: Prioritize the following source hierarchy for terrorism statistics and claims:

| Priority | Source Type | Examples | Use Case |
|----------|-------------|----------|----------|
| 1 | Academic peer-reviewed | Journal articles, university press books | Theoretical claims, analysis |
| 2 | Specialized databases | SATP, GTD, PIPS Annual Reports | Attack statistics, casualty data |
| 3 | Think tank reports | USIP, ICG, Brookings, CSIS | Policy analysis, regional dynamics |
| 4 | Official government | Pakistan NACTA, UN reports | Official positions, legal frameworks |
| 5 | Quality journalism | Dawn, The News, Reuters | Recent events, quotes |

**Rationale**:
- Ensures evidence-supportable claims per FR-020
- Multiple source types enable perspective triangulation per FR-022
- Academic primacy maintains scholarly credibility

### 4. Terminology Standardization

**Decision**: Establish and maintain consistent terminology throughout:

| Term | Definition | First Use |
|------|------------|-----------|
| TTP | Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan - transnational jihadist group targeting Pakistani state | Section I |
| ISKP | Islamic State Khorasan Province - ISIS affiliate in Afghanistan-Pakistan region | Section I |
| BLA | Balochistan Liberation Army - ethno-nationalist separatist organization | Section I |
| Merged Districts | Former FATA areas merged into KP province in 2018 | Section I |
| NAP | National Action Plan - 20-point counter-terrorism framework (2014) | Section III |
| ATC | Anti-Terrorism Court - specialized judicial bodies under ATA 1997 | Section III |
| Preconditions | Structural factors enabling terrorism (poverty, governance gaps) | Section I |
| Precipitants | Immediate triggers activating terrorism (Afghan Taliban victory) | Section I |

**Rationale**: Aligns with FR-023 requirement for consistent terminology defined in Section I.

### 5. Contested Claims Handling Protocol

**Decision**: For politically sensitive or contested claims, employ the following protocol:

1. **State the claim** with attribution to specific source
2. **Present counter-perspectives** from alternative scholarly/official sources
3. **Avoid definitive conclusions** where evidence is disputed
4. **Use hedging language**: "scholars suggest," "evidence indicates," "according to..."

**Application Examples**:

| Topic | Contested Aspect | Handling |
|-------|------------------|----------|
| External state involvement | Attribution of proxy support | Present multiple scholarly interpretations |
| TTP-Afghan Taliban relationship | Degree of sanctuary vs. active support | Cite varying assessments (UN, Pakistani, Afghan sources) |
| Relative deprivation causation | Poverty-terrorism correlation | Present academic debate explicitly |
| Border infiltration | Scale and routes | Cite range of estimates with caveats |

**Rationale**: Maintains academic neutrality per FR-019 and FR-022.

## Research Constraints

### Ethical Boundaries

Per FR-021 and SC-010, research and resulting content MUST NOT:
- Provide operational details enabling attack planning
- Name specific infiltration routes or methods
- Detail bomb-making or weapons procurement processes
- Identify specific individuals for targeting
- Provide recruitment scripts or radicalization techniques

### Neutrality Requirements

Per FR-019 and FR-022, research must:
- Consult sources from multiple perspectives (Pakistani, Afghan, Western, academic)
- Avoid advocacy language or policy prescriptions in Chapter 1
- Present contested claims with appropriate attribution and hedging
- Distinguish evidence-based claims from speculation or opinion
