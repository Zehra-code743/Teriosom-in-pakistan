## 1. Using This Chapter

### Recommended Teaching Sequence

**Prerequisite Check**: Before starting, ensure students have basic understanding of:
- Government structures (what a government does)
- Economic concepts (what poverty and employment mean)
- Geographic awareness (where Pakistan is located)

**Sequencing**:

| Session | Section | Time | Focus | Teaching Mode |
|---------|---------|------|-------|---------------|
| 1 | Index + Section 1 | 20-25 min | Definition, foundation | Direct instruction + discussion |
| 2 | Section 2 | 20-25 min | Political causes | Case study analysis |
| 3 | Section 3 | 20-25 min | Economic causes | Scenario exploration |
| 4 | Section 4 | 20-25 min | Education/misinformation | Media literacy activity |
| 5 | Section 5 | 20-25 min | Extremist tactics | Critical thinking exercise |
| 6 | Review & Assessment | 30-40 min | Synthesis | Assessment + reflection |

**Total**: 5-6 class sessions (or ~150-180 minutes total)

### Session Plans

#### Session 1: Introduction & Definition (Index + Section 1)

**Learning Objective**: Students can define terrorism using three components.

**Opening** (5 min):
- Ask: "What comes to mind when you hear the word 'terrorism'?"
- Record responses (likely: violence, fear, bombs, attacks)
- Explain: "Today we'll learn the formal definition"

**Content** (10 min):
- Read Index together (or assign as homework before Session 1)
- Read Section 1 aloud or independently
- Pause after definition paragraph: Highlight 3 components
  1. Violence/threats
  2. Fear creation
  3. Goals (political/religious/personal)

**Discussion** (5 min):
- Process the Example admonition together
- Discuss: "Why is fear important to terrorism?" (not just physical damage)
- Preview: "Next sections explore WHY terrorism happens"

**Closing** (3 min):
- Reflection prompt from Section 1
- Quick check: "Can you name the three components?"

#### Session 2: Political Causes (Section 2)

**Learning Objective**: Students identify political instability and corruption as terrorism enablers.

**Opening** (3 min):
- Quick review: "What is terrorism?" (reinforce Session 1)
- Bridge: "Today: WHY does terrorism happen? First cause: politics"

**Content** (12 min):
- Read Section 2 independently or in pairs
- As students read, note: Two main concepts (instability + corruption)

**Activity** (8 min):
- **Case Study**: Present hypothetical country scenario:
  - "Country X has had 5 different governments in 10 years..."
  - Ask: "How might this create opportunities for terrorism?"
- Students apply Section 2 concepts to scenario
- Discuss: Weak government → can't control extremists

**Closing** (2 min):
- Reflection prompt from Section 2
- Preview Section 3: "Politics is one cause. Next: economics"

#### Session 3: Economic Causes (Section 3)

**Learning Objective**: Students explain connection between poverty/unemployment and recruitment.

**Opening** (3 min):
- Quick review: "What political problems enable terrorism?"
- Bridge: "Today: How economic problems create vulnerability"

**Content** (12 min):
- Read Section 3 independently
- Focus instruction on "Recruitment Connection" paragraph (critical for SC-003)

**Activity** (8 min):
- **Scenario Exploration**:
  - Give students Hamza and Fatima examples from Section 3
  - Ask: "Why might they be vulnerable to recruitment?"
  - Emphasize: Poverty/unemployment → desperation → groups exploit
- Important discussion point: "This explains cause, doesn't excuse terrorism"

**Closing** (2 min):
- Reflection prompt from Section 3
- Quick check: "How does poverty lead to recruitment?"

#### Session 4: Education & Misinformation (Section 4)

**Learning Objective**: Students distinguish factual information from propaganda.

**Opening** (3 min):
- Ask: "Have you ever seen fake news online?"
- Bridge: "Education gaps make propaganda more effective"

**Content** (10 min):
- Read Section 4 independently
- Emphasize: This section is SOLUTION-ORIENTED (education helps prevent)

**Activity** (10 min):
- **Media Literacy Exercise**:
  - Show 3 headlines (2 factual, 1 sensational/misleading)
  - Students identify which is propaganda
  - Discuss: "How did you know?" (source, language, evidence)
- Connect to Section 4: Critical thinking protects against manipulation

**Closing** (2 min):
- Reflection prompt from Section 4
- Preview: "Last cause: How extremist groups manipulate people"

#### Session 5: Extremist Influence (Section 5)

**Learning Objective**: Students recognize manipulation tactics used by extremist groups.

**Opening** (3 min):
- Ask: "How might someone convince you to do something you don't believe in?"
- Safety note: "We're learning to RECOGNIZE tactics to protect ourselves"

**Content** (12 min):
- Read Section 5 independently
- Note three tactic types: brainwashing, fear, recruitment
- **Important**: Emphasize "Power and Control" section - groups' true goals

**Activity** (8 min):
- **Critical Thinking Exercise**:
  - Present manipulation scenario (from Section 5 examples)
  - Students identify which tactic is being used
  - Discuss: "How could you resist this manipulation?"
- Connect to Sections 2-4: Groups exploit those vulnerabilities

**Closing** (2 min):
- Reflection prompt from Section 5
- Preview assessment: "Next session: review and test understanding"

#### Session 6: Review & Assessment (All Sections)

**Opening** (5 min):
- "We've learned 5 causes of terrorism. Let's review"
- Quick verbal review of each section

**Review Activity** (10 min):
- Create 5-column chart on board (Definition, Political, Economic, Education, Extremism)
- Students contribute key points to each column
- Emphasize interconnections: poverty ↔ education, politics ↔ all causes

**Assessment** (25 min):
- Administer assessment (see Section 2 below for instruments)
- Mix of recall, application, analysis questions

**Closing** (5 min):
- Discuss: "What surprised you most?"
- Preview: "Chapter 2 will explore SOLUTIONS to these causes"

### Discussion Prompts by Section

**Section 1 - Definition**:
- "Why do you think terrorism focuses on creating fear rather than just causing physical damage?"
- "Can you think of examples where fear changed how people behaved?"

**Section 2 - Political**:
- "How might a strong, fair government help prevent terrorism?"
- "What could citizens do if they see corruption making their community less safe?"

**Section 3 - Economic**:
- "If you were designing a program to reduce terrorism, how would you address unemployment?"
- "Why doesn't poverty ALWAYS lead to terrorism?" (not deterministic)

**Section 4 - Education**:
- "What skills do you use to check if information is trustworthy?"
- "How can education help someone resist extremist propaganda?"

**Section 5 - Extremism**:
- "If someone tried to manipulate you using 'us vs. them' language, how would you recognize it?"
- "Why might extremist groups target young people specifically?"

## 2. Assessment Strategies

### Formative Assessment (During Learning)

**Embedded Reflection Prompts**:
- Each section ends with a "Think About It" prompt
- Use these as exit tickets: Students write 2-3 sentence responses before leaving class
- Provides immediate feedback on comprehension

**Discussion Observation**:
- Use discussion prompts (above) to gauge understanding
- Listen for students correctly using terminology (terrorism, instability, corruption, propaganda, manipulation)
- Note misconceptions for re-teaching

**Think-Pair-Share**:
- After reading each section, students:
  1. Think: Write one key point (1 min)
  2. Pair: Share with partner (2 min)
  3. Share: Pairs report to class (2-3 min)

### Summative Assessment Instruments

#### Assessment A: Definition & Recall (Aligns to SC-001)

**Question 1**: Define terrorism in your own words. Your definition should include at least two of these three elements: violence/threats, fear creation, and goals.

**Scoring Rubric**:
- **4 points**: All 3 elements included with clear explanation
- **3 points**: 2 elements included with clear explanation (meets SC-001: 90% target)
- **2 points**: 2 elements included but unclear explanation
- **1 point**: Only 1 element included
- **0 points**: No correct elements

**Success Threshold**: 90% of students score 3 or 4 points (SC-001)

#### Assessment B: Cause Identification (Aligns to SC-002)

**Question 2**: Read the following scenario and identify which THREE causes of terrorism are most relevant:

*"In Region X, the government has changed five times in three years. Many young people cannot find jobs after graduating school. Social media spreads false claims about threats to the community. Local extremist groups offer money to jobless youth."*

Choose three:
- [ ] Political instability
- [ ] Corruption
- [ ] Poverty
- [ ] Unemployment
- [ ] Education gaps
- [ ] Misinformation
- [ ] Extremist manipulation

**Correct Answers**: Political instability, Unemployment, Misinformation (or Extremist manipulation)

**Scoring**:
- **3 points**: All 3 correct (85% target = SC-002)
- **2 points**: 2 correct
- **1 point**: 1 correct
- **0 points**: 0 correct

**Success Threshold**: 85% of students score 3 points (SC-002)

#### Assessment C: Poverty-Recruitment Link (Aligns to SC-003)

**Question 3**: Explain in 3-5 sentences how poverty and unemployment can lead to terrorist recruitment. Include why people might join extremist groups even if they don't agree with the group's beliefs.

**Scoring Rubric**:
- **4 points**: Clear explanation of vulnerability → exploitation → recruitment, mentions desperation/need as motivator
- **3 points**: Explains connection between economic hardship and recruitment (meets SC-003: 80% target)
- **2 points**: Mentions connection but explanation incomplete
- **1 point**: Vague or minimal understanding
- **0 points**: No understanding demonstrated

**Success Threshold**: 80% of students score 3 or 4 points (SC-003)

#### Assessment D: Propaganda Distinction (Aligns to SC-004)

**Question 4**: Read the following statements. Mark each as **FACTUAL** or **PROPAGANDA**:

1. "Unemployment rates among youth aged 18-24 increased by 15% last year." [FACTUAL - has data]
2. "Everyone in our community is under attack and we must fight back now!" [PROPAGANDA - emotional, us-vs-them, urgent]
3. "The extremist group TTP operates primarily in northwestern regions." [FACTUAL - verifiable claim]
4. "Our enemies are destroying our way of life and only we can stop them!" [PROPAGANDA - fear-based, divisive]

**Scoring**:
- **4 points**: All 4 correct (75% target = SC-004)
- **3 points**: 3 correct (meets SC-004)
- **2 points**: 2 correct
- **1 point**: 1 correct
- **0 points**: 0 correct

**Success Threshold**: 75% of students score 3 or 4 points (SC-004)

#### Assessment E: Tactic Identification (Aligns to SC-005)

**Question 5**: Read the scenario and identify at least TWO manipulation tactics being used:

*"An extremist recruiter tells a young person: 'Your community is under threat. The government doesn't care about you. We are your only family now. Everyone outside our group is your enemy. If you don't join us, you're betraying your people.'"*

List tactics you recognize:
1. ___________________________
2. ___________________________

**Possible Correct Answers** (accept any 2+):
- Us-vs-them framing / creating division
- Isolation ("we are your only family")
- Fear-mongering ("under threat")
- Guilt/pressure ("betraying your people")
- Distrust of authorities ("government doesn't care")

**Scoring**:
- **3 points**: 3+ tactics correctly identified
- **2 points**: 2 tactics correctly identified (meets SC-005: 70% target)
- **1 point**: 1 tactic identified
- **0 points**: No tactics identified

**Success Threshold**: 70% of students score 2 or 3 points (SC-005)

### Pre/Post Assessment (Aligns to SC-008)

**Purpose**: Measure 40% comprehension improvement

**Method**:
1. **Pre-test**: Before Session 1, administer short version of Assessments A-E (10 questions total)
2. **Post-test**: After Session 6, administer full Assessments A-E (same questions)
3. **Calculate**: (Post-test average - Pre-test average) / Pre-test average = % improvement
4. **Target**: 40% improvement on average (SC-008)

**Example**:
- Pre-test average: 50% correct
- Post-test average: 70% correct
- Improvement: (70-50)/50 = 40% ✅ Meets SC-008

## 3. Sensitivity Guidance

### General Principles

1. **Assume Diversity**: Your classroom may include:
   - Students from Pakistan or with Pakistani family
   - Students from communities directly affected by terrorism
   - Students with trauma related to violence or conflict
   - Students from various political/religious backgrounds

2. **Create Safety**: Establish ground rules before Session 1:
   - Respectful discussion of sensitive topics
   - Right to pass on discussing personal experiences
   - Confidentiality of stories shared in class
   - Focus on causes/solutions, not blame

3. **Watch for Triggers**: Content may trigger students with:
   - Direct experience of terrorism or violence
   - Family members affected by conflict
   - Refugee/displacement experiences
   - Political persecution experiences

### Sensitivity Notes by Section

#### Section 1 - Definition

**Potential Trigger**: Mentioning terrorism's impact on Pakistan may distress students from affected communities.

**Mitigation**:
- Frame as "learning to understand and prevent" not "dwelling on tragedy"
- Avoid showing images/videos of terrorist attacks
- If student shares personal experience, thank them, validate, and gently redirect to learning objectives

#### Section 2 - Political Problems

**Potential Sensitivity**: Political discussions can become heated, especially if students' families have different political views.

**Mitigation**:
- Emphasize: "We're learning about systemic factors, not blaming specific parties"
- Use hypothetical Country X examples instead of naming specific Pakistani parties/politicians
- If debate gets heated: "These are complex issues. Let's focus on the cause-effect relationships."
- Avoid: Taking sides, criticizing current government, implying one group/party is responsible

**Red Flag**: If student makes extreme political statements (e.g., "All politicians are evil"), respond:
- "It sounds like you feel frustrated with governance. That's understandable and actually relates to what Section 2 explains about how frustration can be exploited."

#### Section 3 - Economic Causes

**Potential Sensitivity**: Students from low-income families may feel stigmatized.

**Mitigation**:
- Emphasize: "Poverty does NOT mean someone will become a terrorist. It's one of many factors that can create vulnerability."
- Frame as: "Understanding economic factors helps us develop solutions like job programs and education access."
- Avoid: Implying poverty causes terrorism (it creates vulnerability that's exploited)
- If student shares family economic hardship: Validate, emphasize that economic challenges are common and don't define a person's future

#### Section 4 - Education & Misinformation

**Potential Sensitivity**: Students with less formal education (or whose parents have less education) may feel judged.

**Mitigation**:
- Emphasize: "Education gaps exist for many reasons - access, conflict, poverty - not because of individual failing."
- Focus on: Education as a RIGHT and a SOLUTION, not a judgment of those without it
- Celebrate: "We're here learning right now, which is exactly how we prevent manipulation"

**Red Flag**: If student shares conspiracy theory or misinformation during discussion:
- Don't shame: "That's an interesting claim. Where did you hear that? How could we check if it's accurate?"
- Model critical thinking: "Let's look for evidence from multiple trustworthy sources."

#### Section 5 - Extremist Influence

**Potential Trigger**: Describing manipulation tactics may remind students of personal experiences with manipulation (not necessarily terrorism-related - could be bullying, abusive relationships, etc.).

**Potential Misuse**: Students could theoretically use this information to manipulate others.

**Mitigation**:
- Opening safety statement: "We're learning to RECOGNIZE tactics to protect ourselves, not to use them on others."
- Emphasize: "These tactics are harmful and wrong. Understanding them makes you less vulnerable."
- If student seems distressed during Section 5: Check in privately after class, offer counselor referral if needed
- If student jokes about using tactics: Serious response: "These cause real harm. We're learning to recognize and resist, not replicate."

**Red Flag**: If student expresses sympathy for extremist groups or recruitment:
- Stay calm, don't overreact
- Ask clarifying questions: "What about that message appeals to you?"
- Reiterate: Section 5 explains that groups' stated goals ≠ true goals (power and control)
- Report to school counselor/administrator per school protocol (varies by region/school policy)

### Handling Trauma Disclosures

**If student discloses personal experience with terrorism/violence**:

1. **Thank and validate**: "Thank you for sharing. That must have been very difficult."
2. **Assess immediate needs**: "Are you okay to continue with class?" (Let them step out if needed)
3. **Don't press for details**: "You don't need to share more unless you want to."
4. **Connect to learning**: "Understanding causes helps us work toward preventing others from experiencing what you did."
5. **Follow up privately**: After class, offer counselor referral
6. **Maintain confidentiality**: Don't share student's disclosure with class without permission

**If multiple students share trauma**:
- Acknowledge this is a significant topic affecting many
- Validate feelings
- Redirect to learning objectives: "The best way we can honor experiences is to understand causes and work toward solutions."

### Managing Political Discussions

**Classroom Norms** (establish in Session 1):
- "We will discuss political factors without blaming specific groups"
- "We will respect diverse political views in this room"
- "We will focus on cause-effect relationships, not political advocacy"

**If student makes inflammatory political statement**:
- Acknowledge: "I hear you have strong feelings about this."
- Redirect: "For this class, let's focus on how the factor (instability/corruption) enables terrorism, not which specific party is responsible."
- Reframe: "Productive discussion is about understanding systems, not assigning blame."

**If you (educator) have strong political views**:
- Be aware of your biases
- Present multiple perspectives
- Avoid: Criticizing government, parties, or political figures
- Remember: Your role is educational facilitator, not political commentator

### Addressing Misinformation from Students

**During Section 4 discussion, student may share misinformation they've encountered.**

**Response Strategy**:
1. **Don't shame**: "That's a claim I've heard circulating. Let's examine it."
2. **Apply critical thinking**: "What evidence supports this? What sources say otherwise?"
3. **Model fact-checking**: "Let's check multiple credible sources together."
4. **Reinforce lesson**: "This is exactly what Section 4 teaches - how to evaluate claims."

**Examples**:
- **Claim**: "Terrorism in Pakistan is only because of [specific group]"
  - **Response**: "Section 2-5 show multiple causes. It's rarely just one factor."

- **Claim**: "All [ethnic/religious group] support terrorism"
  - **Response**: "That's a harmful generalization. Section 5 explains extremist groups are a small minority, not representative of entire communities."

### When to Seek Additional Support

**Involve school counselor/administrator if**:
- Student expresses intent to harm self or others
- Student expresses sympathy for violence or extremist ideology
- Student discloses abuse or safety concern
- Student shows signs of trauma (dissociation, panic, severe distress)
- Student's comments suggest they're being recruited or radicalized

**Trust your professional judgment. When in doubt, consult.**

## 4. Differentiation Tips

### For Struggling Readers (Below Grade 7)

**Scaffolding Strategies**:
1. **Pre-teach vocabulary**: Before each session, review key terms
   - Session 1: terrorism, violence, fear, goals
   - Session 2: instability, corruption, governance
   - Session 3: poverty, unemployment, recruitment
   - Session 4: propaganda, misinformation, awareness
   - Session 5: manipulation, brainwashing, tactics

2. **Chunking**: Break each section into smaller parts
   - Read 1-2 paragraphs, stop, discuss
   - Use guiding questions: "What does this paragraph tell us?"

3. **Visual aids**: Create graphic organizers
   - Section 1: Definition wheel (3 components)
   - Sections 2-5: Cause → Effect chains
   - Overall: 5-column chart of all causes

4. **Audio support**: Provide text-to-speech option for students who benefit from hearing content

5. **Simplified summaries**: After each section, provide 3-4 sentence summary in simpler language

**Modified Assessment**:
- Provide word bank for short answer questions
- Allow oral responses instead of written for some questions
- Reduce number of questions while maintaining coverage of key concepts

### For Advanced Students (Above Grade 10)

**Extension Activities**:
1. **Research Projects**:
   - Compare terrorism in Pakistan to another region (e.g., Middle East, Africa)
   - Investigate a specific factor (e.g., "Role of Social Media in Radicalization")
   - Analyze effectiveness of counter-terrorism policies

2. **Critical Analysis**:
   - "Section 2-5 explain causes. Rank them by importance. Defend your ranking."
   - "Design a prevention program that addresses at least 3 causes."
   - "Critique: Does this chapter oversimplify? What's missing?"

3. **Primary Source Analysis**:
   - Read academic excerpts from terrorism studies literature
   - Compare student chapter to academic Chapter 1 (if available)
   - Analyze news articles about Pakistan for bias and accuracy

4. **Solutions Preview**:
   - "Based on the causes in Chapter 1, propose solutions for Chapter 2"
   - Create prevention campaign targeting one cause

**Advanced Assessment**:
- Essay questions instead of short answer
- Require synthesis across multiple sections
- Ask for evidence beyond the text

### For English Language Learners (ELL)

**Language Support**:
1. **Glossary**: Provide definitions for key terms in students' home language (e.g., Urdu)
2. **Sentence Frames**: Help structure responses
   - "Terrorism is defined as __________, __________, and __________."
   - "One cause of terrorism is __________ because __________."
   - "An example of this is __________."

3. **Visual supports**: Use images/diagrams to convey concepts
   - Political instability: Image of government building with "changing leadership" label
   - Poverty: Graph showing resource scarcity

4. **Pair with language buddy**: Partner ELL student with proficient English speaker for discussion activities

5. **Extended time**: Allow more time for reading and assessment

**Modified Assessment**:
- Allow responses in home language (if educator can assess or get translation)
- Accept shorter responses demonstrating understanding
- Provide bilingual word bank

### For Students with Trauma History

**Modifications**:
1. **Pre-notification**: Let student know ahead of time: "Next week we'll discuss sensitive topics. Let me know if you need support."
2. **Exit option**: Allow student to step out if content is triggering, with alternative assignment
3. **Alternative examples**: If Pakistan-specific content is triggering, allow student to engage with more general examples
4. **Flexible assessment**: If assessment questions are triggering, provide alternative questions covering same content

**Support**:
- Check in individually before/after sessions
- Coordinate with school counselor
- Provide resource list (helplines, counseling services)

### For Different Learning Styles

**Visual Learners**:
- Provide graphic organizers for each section
- Use Venn diagrams to show overlapping causes
- Create flowcharts for cause → effect relationships

**Auditory Learners**:
- Read sections aloud as class
- Use podcasts/interviews (if appropriate content available)
- Encourage Think-Pair-Share discussions

**Kinesthetic Learners**:
- Role-play scenarios from examples
- Create physical models (e.g., puzzle pieces showing interconnected causes)
- Use movement activities (e.g., students physically group by which cause they think is most important, then defend)

## 5. Review Requirements

### Three-Tier Review Process

All content must pass three sequential reviews before publication:
1. **Internal Validation** (self-check against contracts)
2. **Educator Review** (SC-006: age-appropriateness)
3. **SME Review** (SC-007: factual accuracy + SC-010: sensitivity)

---

### Tier 1: Internal Validation Checklist

**Purpose**: Ensure content meets contract specifications before external review

**Reviewer**: Content creator or instructional designer

**Timeline**: Complete before submitting to Tier 2

**Checklist**:

#### For Each Section (01-05):
- [ ] Word count within target range (±50 words acceptable)
- [ ] Flesch-Kincaid grade level within target for section
- [ ] All required content elements present (per contracts/README.md)
- [ ] Standard section structure followed (Introduction, concepts, impact, summary, reflection)
- [ ] Examples follow example template (setup, vulnerability, connection)
- [ ] Admonitions properly formatted (`:::info`, `:::tip`)
- [ ] Frontmatter complete and accurate
- [ ] Links functional (internal cross-references)
- [ ] No [NEEDS CLARIFICATION] markers remain
- [ ] No typos or grammatical errors

#### For Index (index.md):
- [ ] All 5 sections linked with descriptions
- [ ] Learning objectives stated and aligned to SC-001 through SC-005
- [ ] Chapter summary present
- [ ] Word count: 300-500
- [ ] FK grade level: 7.0-8.0
- [ ] Frontmatter complete

#### Cross-Section Checks:
- [ ] Section 1 establishes foundation for 2-5
- [ ] Terminology consistent across sections (terrorism definition)
- [ ] Progressive complexity: FK level increases from Section 1 to 5
- [ ] Total word count: 3000-4000 (all files combined)
- [ ] Thematic coverage: All five causes addressed

#### Safety Checks:
- [ ] No graphic violence descriptions (FR-012)
- [ ] No tactical operational details (FR-012, SC-009)
- [ ] No named terrorist groups in student-facing content
- [ ] Academic neutrality maintained (FR-004)
- [ ] Examples show vulnerability, not recruitment outcomes

**Outcome**: If all items checked, proceed to Tier 2. If failures, revise and re-check.

### Tier 3: Subject Matter Expert (SME) + Sensitivity Review (SC-007, SC-010)

**Purpose**: Validate factual accuracy, academic neutrality, and cultural appropriateness

**Reviewers**:
1. **SME**: Terrorism studies expert, South Asia specialist, or political science professor
2. **Sensitivity Reviewer**: Pakistani educator, cultural consultant, or trauma-informed education specialist

**Timeline**: 1 week turnaround per reviewer (may be parallel or sequential)

**SME Qualifications**:
- Advanced degree in terrorism studies, political science, South Asian studies, or related field
- Published research or professional experience in Pakistan/South Asia
- Familiarity with academic neutrality standards

**Sensitivity Reviewer Qualifications**:
- Pakistani national or Pakistani diaspora educator preferred
- Experience with trauma-informed education
- Understanding of Pakistani cultural context and diverse regional perspectives

#### SME Review Checklist (SC-007):

**Factual Accuracy**:
- [ ] Terrorism definition accurate and aligns with academic consensus
- [ ] Political factors (instability, corruption) accurately represented
- [ ] Economic factors (poverty, unemployment) evidence-based
- [ ] Education-terrorism link supported by research
- [ ] Extremist tactic descriptions factually accurate
- [ ] Pakistan context accurate (no outdated or false information)
- [ ] Statistics/data cited are accurate (if any)

**Academic Rigor**:
- [ ] Multi-causality acknowledged (not oversimplified)
- [ ] Complexity respected while remaining accessible
- [ ] Avoids deterministic language (e.g., "poverty causes terrorism" vs. "creates vulnerability")
- [ ] Distinguishes correlation from causation
- [ ] Acknowledges debates/uncertainties where relevant

**Academic Neutrality**:
- [ ] No political bias (toward/against specific parties, governments)
- [ ] No ideological bias (religious, ethnic, sectarian)
- [ ] Multiple perspectives represented fairly
- [ ] Avoids blame language
- [ ] Focuses on systemic factors, not individual/cultural deficiency

**Safety**:
- [ ] No tactical information that could be misused (SC-009)
- [ ] Manipulation tactics described conceptually, not operationally
- [ ] Content cannot serve as "how-to" guide for harmful actions

**Overall SME Recommendation**:
- [ ] **Approved: Factually accurate and academically sound**
- [ ] **Approved with minor corrections** (specify: __________________)
- [ ] **Major revisions needed** (specify: __________________)
- [ ] **Not suitable for publication** (specify why: __________________)
