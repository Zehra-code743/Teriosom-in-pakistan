## Contract 2: Section 1 - Understanding Terrorism (01-understanding-terrorism.md)

### Scope
Foundation section defining terrorism, explaining psychological impact beyond physical violence, and establishing Pakistan context. **Must be read first.**

### Specifications

| Attribute | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Word Count** | 400-600 words |
| **Reading Level** | FK 7.0-8.0 (foundation, most accessible) |
| **Sidebar Position** | 2 |
| **Est. Reading Time** | 3-4 minutes |
| **Success Criteria** | SC-001 (90% definition accuracy) |

### Required Content Elements

1. **Introduction** (2-3 sentences)
   - Activating statement: Ask what students think terrorism means
   - Learning objective: "You will learn to define terrorism and understand its impact"

2. **Definition of Terrorism** (1-2 paragraphs)
   - Three components: violence/threats, creation of fear, goals (political/religious/personal)
   - Explain each component clearly
   - Emphasize terrorism is more than just violence - it's about psychological impact

3. **:::info Example** (1 admonition)
   - Hypothetical scenario showing how terrorism uses fear to control
   - E.g., "When a group threatens people in a region to force them to follow certain rules..."
   - 2-3 sentences, no graphic violence

4. **Why Terrorism Matters** (1 paragraph)
   - Not just physical destruction
   - Creates fear in entire communities
   - Affects innocent people who have nothing to do with conflicts

5. **Pakistan Context** (1-2 paragraphs)
   - Terrorism is global problem but Pakistan especially affected
   - Thousands of people impacted
   - Sets stage for understanding WHY this happens (Sections 2-5)

6. **Summary** (3-4 bullet points)
   - Terrorism = violence + fear + goals
   - Psychological impact beyond physical
   - Pakistan significantly affected
   - Next sections explore WHY

7. **:::tip Think About It** (1 admonition)
   - Reflection question: "Why do you think terrorism uses fear instead of just violence?"
   - Align to SC-001 (understanding definition components)

### Acceptance Criteria

- ✅ Definition includes all 3 components (violence/threats, fear, goals)
- ✅ Pakistan context established without graphic details
- ✅ 1 example admonition (terrorism concept illustrated)
- ✅ 1 reflection prompt (tests definition comprehension)
- ✅ Word count: 400-600
- ✅ FK grade level: 7.0-8.0
- ✅ Frontmatter complete (sidebar_position: 2)
- ✅ Educator review: Definition clear for grades 7-10
- ✅ SME review: Definition factually accurate

## Contract 3: Section 2 - Political Problems (02-political-problems.md)

### Scope
Explain how political instability and corruption create conditions enabling terrorist groups to operate and recruit in Pakistan.

### Specifications

| Attribute | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Word Count** | 500-700 words |
| **Reading Level** | FK 8.0-9.0 (intermediate) |
| **Sidebar Position** | 3 |
| **Est. Reading Time** | 4-5 minutes |
| **Success Criteria** | SC-002 (85% cause identification - political factor) |

### Required Content Elements

1. **Introduction** (2-3 sentences)
   - Activating: "Have you heard about elections or government changes in the news?"
   - Learning objective: "Understand how political problems create opportunities for terrorism"

2. **Political Instability** (2 paragraphs)
   - Definition: Frequent government changes, weak leadership
   - Effect: People lose trust in system
   - Connection to terrorism: Weak government can't control extremist groups effectively

3. **:::info Example** (1 admonition)
   - Scenario about government instability
   - E.g., "When a government changes frequently, law enforcement becomes less effective..."
   - 2-3 sentences

4. **Corruption** (2 paragraphs)
   - Definition: Officials take bribes, don't enforce rules
   - Effect: Citizens become frustrated, services fail
   - Connection to terrorism: Frustration makes violent solutions seem appealing

5. **:::info Example** (1 admonition)
   - Scenario about corruption impact
   - E.g., "If people believe elections are unfair or only certain groups benefit..."
   - 2-3 sentences

6. **Impact on Pakistan** (1-2 paragraphs)
   - How political issues specifically manifest in Pakistan
   - Why this makes terrorism harder to combat
   - NOT partisan/blame language - systemic factors

7. **Summary** (3-4 bullet points)
   - Political instability weakens control
   - Corruption frustrates citizens
   - Both create environment for terrorism
   - Solutions require better governance (preview Chapter 2)

8. **:::tip Think About It** (1 admonition)
   - "How might strong, fair governance help prevent terrorism?"
   - Tests understanding of political factor (SC-002)

### Acceptance Criteria

- ✅ Both political instability AND corruption explained
- ✅ Clear connection to terrorism for each factor
- ✅ 2 example admonitions (one per concept)
- ✅ Academic neutrality maintained (FR-004) - no political bias
- ✅ Pakistan context included without blame language
- ✅ Word count: 500-700
- ✅ FK grade level: 8.0-9.0
- ✅ Frontmatter complete (sidebar_position: 3)
- ✅ Educator review: Politically sensitive content handled appropriately
- ✅ SME review: Accurate representation of political factors

## Contract 4: Section 3 - Poverty and Unemployment (03-poverty-unemployment.md)

### Scope
Explain how economic hardship (poverty, unemployment) creates vulnerability that extremist groups exploit for recruitment.

### Specifications

| Attribute | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Word Count** | 500-700 words |
| **Reading Level** | FK 8.0-9.0 (intermediate) |
| **Sidebar Position** | 4 |
| **Est. Reading Time** | 4-5 minutes |
| **Success Criteria** | SC-002 (cause identification) + SC-003 (80% poverty-recruitment link) |

### Required Content Elements

1. **Introduction** (2-3 sentences)
   - Activating: "What happens when families struggle to afford basic needs?"
   - Learning objective: "Understand how economic problems make people vulnerable to extremism"

2. **Poverty** (2 paragraphs)
   - Definition: Lack of food, clothing, shelter (basic needs)
   - Effect: Families become desperate, vulnerable
   - Connection to terrorism: Extremist groups offer money/resources

3. **:::info Example** (1 admonition)
   - Hypothetical person/family in poverty
   - E.g., "Fatima's family cannot afford to keep her in school because they need money for food..."
   - Shows vulnerability without depicting recruitment (before, not after)

4. **Unemployment** (2 paragraphs)
   - Definition: Young people cannot find jobs
   - Effect: Frustration, hopelessness, lack of purpose
   - Connection to terrorism: Groups offer purpose and income

5. **:::info Example** (1 admonition)
   - Hypothetical unemployed youth
   - E.g., "Hamza graduated but has no job prospects. When an extremist group offers him money..."
   - Focus on vulnerability point, not violent actions

6. **The Recruitment Connection** (1-2 paragraphs)
   - **THIS IS KEY FOR SC-003**
   - Explicitly explain: Poverty/unemployment → vulnerability → groups exploit → recruitment
   - Emphasize: People don't support ideology, they need money/purpose
   - Important: This doesn't excuse terrorism, but explains one cause

7. **Impact on Pakistan** (1 paragraph)
   - Economic statistics (high youth unemployment rates, poverty levels)
   - How this fuels recruitment in certain regions
   - Aggregate data, not graphic stories

8. **Summary** (3-4 bullet points)
   - Poverty creates desperation
   - Unemployment creates hopelessness
   - Both make recruitment easier
   - Economic solutions part of prevention (Chapter 2)

9. **:::tip Think About It** (1 admonition)
   - "Why might someone join an extremist group for money even if they don't agree with the group's beliefs?"
   - Tests SC-003 (poverty-recruitment link comprehension)

### Acceptance Criteria

- ✅ Both poverty AND unemployment explained as separate factors
- ✅ Recruitment connection explicitly stated (SC-003 requirement)
- ✅ 2 example admonitions (poverty + unemployment scenarios)
- ✅ Examples show vulnerability, NOT violent actions (FR-012)
- ✅ No stigmatization of poor/unemployed people
- ✅ Word count: 500-700
- ✅ FK grade level: 8.0-9.0
- ✅ Frontmatter complete (sidebar_position: 4)
- ✅ Educator review: Sensitive to students from low-income backgrounds
- ✅ SME review: Economic factors accurately represented

## Contract 5: Section 4 - Education and Awareness (04-education-awareness.md)

### Scope
Explain how lack of education and misinformation make people susceptible to manipulation and extremist propaganda; introduce prevention through awareness.

### Specifications

| Attribute | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Word Count** | 500-700 words |
| **Reading Level** | FK 8.0-10.0 (increasing to advanced) |
| **Sidebar Position** | 5 |
| **Est. Reading Time** | 4-5 minutes |
| **Success Criteria** | SC-002 (cause identification) + SC-004 (75% propaganda distinction) |

### Required Content Elements

1. **Introduction** (2-3 sentences)
   - Activating: "How do you know if information you see online is true or fake?"
   - Learning objective: "Learn how education gaps and misinformation contribute to terrorism"

2. **Lack of Education** (2 paragraphs)
   - Problem: People without formal education may misunderstand religion, politics
   - Effect: More susceptible to manipulation, can't critically evaluate claims
   - Connection to terrorism: Groups exploit gaps to spread extremist ideas

3. **:::info Example** (1 admonition)
   - Hypothetical person leaving school early
   - E.g., "Ali left school in 8th grade and now gets all his information from one source..."
   - Shows how education gaps create vulnerability

4. **Misinformation and Propaganda** (2-3 paragraphs)
   - Definition: Fake news, manipulated information, propaganda on social media
   - How it spreads: Social media, word of mouth, targeted messaging
   - Effect: People believe false narratives about enemies, threats, grievances
   - Connection to terrorism: Groups use propaganda to recruit and justify violence

5. **:::info Example** (1 admonition)
   - Scenario about misinformation spreading
   - E.g., "A video circulates on social media making false claims about a group..."
   - Shows propaganda technique WITHOUT graphic content

6. **The Power of Awareness** (1-2 paragraphs)
   - **SOLUTION-ORIENTED (bridges to Chapter 2)**
   - Education teaches critical thinking
   - Awareness campaigns help people identify propaganda
   - Schools, TV, social media can spread counter-narratives
   - Preview that Chapter 2 expands on education solutions

7. **Impact on Pakistan** (1 paragraph)
   - Education access challenges in certain regions
   - Social media usage and misinformation spread
   - Efforts to improve awareness (mention constructive work)

8. **Summary** (3-4 bullet points)
   - Education gaps create vulnerability
   - Misinformation spreads extremist ideas
   - Awareness and critical thinking are protective factors
   - Education is both cause AND solution

9. **:::tip Think About It** (1 admonition)
   - "If you see a sensational claim on social media, what are three ways you could check if it's true?"
   - Tests SC-004 (ability to distinguish facts from propaganda)

### Acceptance Criteria

- ✅ Both education gaps AND misinformation explained
- ✅ Solution-oriented language introduced (education as prevention)
- ✅ 2 example admonitions (education gap + misinformation scenarios)
- ✅ Teaches propaganda identification skills (SC-004)
- ✅ No stigmatization of people with less formal education
- ✅ Acknowledges positive education efforts in Pakistan
- ✅ Word count: 500-700
- ✅ FK grade level: 8.0-10.0
- ✅ Frontmatter complete (sidebar_position: 5)
- ✅ Educator review: Empowers students with critical thinking tools
- ✅ SME review: Accurate representation of education-terrorism link

## Contract 6: Section 5 - Extremist Group Influence (05-extremist-influence.md)

### Scope
Explain how extremist groups use manipulation (brainwashing), fear tactics, and recruitment strategies to spread terrorism, with focus on recognizing these tactics.

### Specifications

| Attribute | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Word Count** | 500-700 words |
| **Reading Level** | FK 9.0-10.0 (most advanced section) |
| **Sidebar Position** | 6 |
| **Est. Reading Time** | 4-5 minutes |
| **Success Criteria** | SC-002 (cause identification) + SC-005 (70% tactic identification) + SC-009 (no tactical details) |

### Required Content Elements

1. **Introduction** (2-3 sentences)
   - Activating: "How might someone convince you to do something you don't believe in?"
   - Learning objective: "Recognize manipulation tactics used by extremist groups"
   - **Safety note**: Learning to recognize is for self-protection, not to use

2. **Brainwashing / Manipulation** (2 paragraphs)
   - Definition: Psychological manipulation to change beliefs
   - Techniques: Repetition, isolation, us-vs-them framing
   - Effect: People adopt harmful ideology even if it contradicts their prior values
   - Connection to terrorism: Groups use this to justify violence

3. **:::info Example** (1 admonition)
   - Hypothetical manipulation scenario
   - E.g., "An extremist group repeatedly tells young people that 'everyone is against them'..."
   - Focus on manipulation method, not violent outcome

4. **Fear Tactics** (2 paragraphs)
   - Definition: Using threats, violence, or intimidation to control
   - Methods: Public threats, propaganda showing violence, social pressure
   - Effect: Communities become afraid to resist or report
   - Connection to terrorism: Fear enables groups to operate and recruit

5. **:::info Example** (1 admonition)
   - Hypothetical fear tactic scenario
   - E.g., "A group makes threats against families who refuse to cooperate..."
   - Describes tactic conceptually, NO graphic violence

6. **Recruitment Patterns** (2 paragraphs)
   - Who they target: Youth, vulnerable people, those in Sections 3-4 situations
   - Where: Schools, mosques, communities, online
   - Why groups succeed: Exploit vulnerabilities from Sections 2-4 (politics, economy, education)
   - **CRITICAL FOR SC-009**: Do NOT describe operational details, specific methods that could be replicated

7. **The True Goal: Power and Control** (1 paragraph)
   - Groups claim to fight for justice/religion/community
   - Real goal: Power and control over people and territory
   - Violence serves their interests, not community welfare
   - Important for students to understand: Stated aims ≠ true aims

8. **Impact on Pakistan** (1 paragraph)
   - How these tactics manifest in Pakistan context
   - Communities affected by fear and manipulation
   - Society becomes divided (trust erodes)
   - Aggregate impact, not specific stories

9. **Summary** (3-4 bullet points)
   - Brainwashing changes beliefs through manipulation
   - Fear tactics control communities
   - Recruitment targets vulnerable people (Sections 2-4 factors)
   - True goal is power, not stated ideology
   - Recognizing tactics helps resist them

10. **:::tip Think About It** (1 admonition)
    - "If someone tried to convince you that 'everyone is against our community,' what are some ways you could check if that's really true?"
    - Tests SC-005 (identify manipulation tactics)

### Acceptance Criteria

- ✅ All three tactic types explained (brainwashing, fear, recruitment)
- ✅ Clear distinction between stated vs. true goals of groups
- ✅ 2 example admonitions (manipulation + fear scenarios)
- ✅ Examples describe tactics conceptually, NO operational details (SC-009, FR-012)
- ✅ Empowers students to recognize, not instructions to replicate
- ✅ Connects back to vulnerabilities from Sections 2-4
- ✅ Word count: 500-700
- ✅ FK grade level: 9.0-10.0
- ✅ Frontmatter complete (sidebar_position: 6)
- ✅ Educator review: Appropriate for grades 7-10, no trauma triggers
- ✅ SME review: Accurate tactic descriptions, no harmful details
- ✅ **Safety review**: Content cannot be misused (SC-009 critical check)
